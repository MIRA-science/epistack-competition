Schema at a glance

One source of truth; everything else generated.

The canonical grammar is mira.yaml (LinkML). From it, LinkML generates mira.shacl (machine-checkable conformance), mira.ttl (RDF), and mira.jsonld (the JSON-LD context that is the wire format). Hand-editing the generated artifacts is a bug; edit the source and regenerate.

mira.yaml→gen-shacl→mira.shacl+mira.jsonld+mira.ttl

Node types: Question · Claim · Evidence · Study · Protocol · Request · SourceDocument. Core slots used on this site: observationBase · observationStatement · is_grounded_in/grounds · follows · sourceDocument · addresses · supports/opposes/informs · request_for/request_target. Canonical source: github.com/MIRA-science/schema.

Prior art

Extending a lineage, not reinventing it.

Prior artShared ideaWhat MIRA adds
NanopublicationsThe reified assertion — a statement that carries its own provenance and author.A question layer — open unknowns as durable, first-class nodes — and the specific relation this observation is about that claim, carried as its own relation slot (the observationStatement / observationBase split).
AIF (Argument Interchange Format)Argument structure — supports and attacks between claims.
PROV-OProvenance — who produced what, from what, when.

MIRA is compatible with all three; the additions are the question layer and the observation-about-claim relation. Most systems that emit claims-with-source-spans already produce, in effect, an Evidence + observationStatement + sourceDocument triple — the mapping is short, which is the whole bet of a shared schema.
