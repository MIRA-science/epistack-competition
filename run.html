Run it, don’t just read it

The prototypes — public, and marked for how real they are.

Everything here is public — most of it under github.com/MIRA-science, the rest hosted by the people who built it at the kickoff. Each entry says what it is, points to a link, and what to try — and carries a status, because this page is also the provenance. For a short demo video of each, see What we built at the MIRA Kickoff.

The canonical LinkML grammar — every node and edge on this site is defined in it. SHACL is generated from the source; the Turtle and JSON-LD context are maintained alongside it.

MIRA-extractionshipped

AI-assisted MIRAfication of a source — the MIRA-fier of the kickoff write-up. Emits schema-conformant JSON-LD (<name>.mira.jsonld) — the wire format the viewers and the KOI envelope speak — with a verbatim provenance.excerpt on every node (the receipts), dangling and ungrammatical edges reported instead of dropped, and the source → study → grounds → evidence spine enforced.

What to try: MIRAfy a source and diff the Initial AI draft nodes against what a human would advance; the emitted .mira.jsonld is what moves downstream.

MIROXA · MIRA in Stencilashipped · in Stencila

MIRA inside the existing OXA standard. Stencila, an open-source publishing platform, reads and writes Questions, Claims, Evidence, Protocols and Requests — authored as MyST directives, Quarto divs or Stencila Markdown blocks — and converts them to and from MIRA JSON-LD (.mira.jsonld). A second, independent authoring tool on the same wire format.

What to try: follow the Stencila docs to export a marked-up document with stencila graph <doc>.myst <doc>.mira.jsonld.

stencila.io/docs/formats/mira ↗
koi-net-mira-prototypeshipped

The KOI cross-organization envelope — operational. Different orgs’ agents and tools read from and write to the same KOI node, and it ingests the JSON-LD that MIRA-extraction emits. Our working MIRA KOI node contains unpublished scientific results by our users, and so isn’t yet public — and even there, each record is a pointer to its artifact, not a copy of it. Still designed, not built: federation across schemas and a public network at scale.

What to try: stand up your own KOI node to push and pull MIRA nodes across the network.

RRGI · the resilient research graphshipped · live

A public graph of MIRA records on AT Protocol and IPFS, built by SciOS: papers as bundles of signed records, questions and evidence added by signed-in researchers, claims endorsed as believed, verified or qualified, and a PDF extractor that turns a paper into records. It maps MIRA’s node types and relations onto its own lexicon and adds a few — proposed back to the schema in the open.

What to try: browse the public graph without signing in; open a paper and follow a claim to its evidence.

Deltabaydesigned · UI mockup

A funder-facing view from the kickoff: open scientific debates where every claim is backed by evidence, and endorsements are scored to show how much each piece of evidence moves the answer to a question. It is a lens applied on top of the graph — the scoring is the application’s, not the schema’s; MIRA itself still computes nothing.

What to try: open a debate and trace a claim to the evidence behind it.

schema/atprotodesigned

A draft ATProto lexicon binding (science.mira.*) for public discourse-graph nodes — the intended public layer. Self-labeled DRAFT / PROPOSAL: not adopted, not published, not on the network. A working proof of concept on AT Protocol exists separately — RRGI, above, on its own lexicon.

What to try: read the lexicons as a concrete direction; treat them as a proposal pending schema-team review, not a shipped surface.

The shape of it

One shared transport. A closed loop.

Both authoring paths write the same JSON-LD into one KOI node — the shared cross-org transport every tool reads and writes. And the graph isn’t a dead end: it surfaces its own open questions as Requests, which spin out new studies whose evidence flows back in. Data in across the top; the next study out across the bottom.
